# Proper Infinite Horizons — Speedrun Salvage Principle

## Core rule

The pack must not enforce a minimum amount of playtime before endgame crafting becomes possible.

The expected route is a long industrial build in which the player constructs each production layer deliberately. However, an exceptionally skilled and fortunate player should have a slim but genuine possibility of spawning into a favorable region, reading the available structures correctly, scavenging complementary parts and materials, repairing only the machinery they need, and crafting an endgame item within roughly one hour.

This is not intended to be the normal route. It is a high-knowledge, high-attention, high-luck route.

## What the speedrun fantasy rewards

A fast run should reward four things at once:

1. **World reading** — recognizing silhouettes, structure types, broken processing lines, and likely salvage before entering.
2. **Mod knowledge** — knowing which recovered component bypasses which crafting bottleneck.
3. **Route judgment** — deciding immediately whether a ruin is worth repairing, stripping, or abandoning.
4. **Execution** — moving, fighting, inventory-managing, and assembling quickly enough to convert luck into progress.

Luck creates the opening. Knowledge turns it into a run.

## No hard time gates

Avoid progression requirements that exist only to delay the player:

- mandatory waiting periods;
- arbitrary quest completion locks;
- forced ownership of every lower-tier machine;
- recipes requiring a machine only because it belongs to an earlier chapter;
- unskippable material loops when the same material can reasonably be scavenged;
- stage locks that reject already-obtained endgame components.

A player who legitimately acquires the required materials, power, machine access, and recipe knowledge may proceed.

## The lucky-hour route

A viable extreme-luck route might look like this:

1. Spawn near a village and scavenge iron, food, a bed, and basic tools.
2. Spot a collapsed workshop and repair enough rotational machinery to process early materials.
3. Find a rail depot, pump station, or substation containing complementary components instead of duplicates.
4. Recover a rare intact machine block or nearly complete machine line from a dangerous structure.
5. Use an advanced ruin to obtain one or two critical circuits, processors, coils, or alloys that normally require a long intermediate chain.
6. Salvage high-value raw materials from structure storage, broken multiblocks, or rare treasure caches.
7. Reconnect found infrastructure, craft the few missing links, and produce an endgame item.

The route only works when several independent rare opportunities align.

## Structure design for route compression

Custom structures should contain three forms of progression value:

### 1. Visible infrastructure

Structures visually demonstrate functional machine layouts, but with missing links:

- broken conveyor spans;
- absent shafts or cogwheels;
- severed fluid pipes;
- missing power connectors;
- incomplete multiblock walls;
- disabled inputs or outputs;
- empty fuel or reagent storage;
- machines facing the wrong direction after collapse.

The player should be able to infer the original process and decide whether repairing it is faster than rebuilding it.

### 2. Complementary salvage

Nearby structures should not all draw from the same generic pool. Their identities should create possible routes:

- workshops provide mechanical transmission and Create components;
- pump stations provide pipes, tanks, pumps, rubber, and fluid components;
- substations provide coils, connectors, capacitors, and power components;
- machine halls provide casings, alloys, circuits, and occasional machine blocks;
- data centers provide AE2 processors, printed components, storage parts, and logic infrastructure;
- depots provide tracks, belts, loaders, crates, and logistics components.

A lucky route emerges when different structures provide different missing pieces.

### 3. Jackpot finds

Dangerous and rare structures may contain extremely valuable finds:

- an intact lower- or mid-tier machine;
- a partially assembled advanced machine line;
- a rare processor or control circuit;
- an expensive coil, alloy, casing, or mechanism;
- enough uncommon material to skip one production bottleneck;
- a map or record pointing directly to another complementary site.

Jackpots should compress a chain, not automatically finish the game. Even the best find should require the player to understand what it enables.

## Balancing target

The ordinary player should experience custom structures as useful discoveries that shorten individual steps.

The expert player should see route possibilities.

The speedrunner should occasionally see a seed where the entire progression graph appears to collapse into one narrow, executable path.

That possibility should be rare enough to feel legendary, but common enough that repeated runs can realistically hunt for it. The target is not a precise seed percentage yet; it should be measured through repeated fresh-world route tests after the first structure set exists.

## Failure conditions

The design has failed if:

- endgame within an hour is mathematically impossible regardless of seed or skill;
- a single chest grants the whole progression chain;
- ordinary exploration makes crafting most components pointless;
- jackpot loot is required for normal progression;
- structures contain machines but do not visually teach how they connect;
- speed depends only on random drops rather than player recognition and execution;
- quests prevent the use of legitimately scavenged advanced components.

## Testing requirement

Every major progression revision should be evaluated with two routes:

1. **Builder route:** progress normally, constructing the intended industrial chain.
2. **Scavenger speed route:** exploit every legitimate structure, machine, component, and material shortcut available.

The builder route tests clarity and pacing. The scavenger route tests whether knowledge and luck can still break the expected schedule without breaking the game.
